Flow cytometry analysis that gates your whole plate at once

FlowMate takes FCS files from raw data to a graph with the statistics done. Draw a gate once and every well updates live. Compensate, measure and compare conditions with t tests and ANOVA — with nothing to install, and your data never leaving your computer.

What FlowMate does

Five steps — Load, Plate, Gate, Measure, Analysis — built around how plate experiments are actually run.

One gate, every well

Polygon, rectangle, ellipse, quadrant and range gates, plus And / Or / Not. Drag a gate and the whole plate, its heat map and a filmstrip of every well follow live. Adjust one well where it needs it.

Knows about plates

Paint conditions onto wells, mark controls, exclude a bad well with a reason, and measure one row per well. Tubes work too.

Compensation

The files' own matrix, a matrix computed from single-stain controls, or one you import. Spectral unmixing from single stains is in beta.

Statistics done for you

Bar, dot, box or paired graphs with SD or SEM, the right test chosen for you — t test, one-way ANOVA, Tukey or Dunnett — and a nested option that uses every cell without counting cells as replicates.

Opens FlowJo and Gating-ML

Import FlowJo 10 workspaces (.wsp) with their gates, groups and compensation; the import report shows FlowJo's count next to FlowMate's for every population. Gating-ML 2.0 in and out.

Private and offline

FCS files are read inside your browser tab — there is no server. Save analyses with their data in the browser, or download FlowMate as one HTML file that works offline.

From gates to a figure with the P values

Pick a Measure column, choose the graph and the comparisons, and FlowMate runs the test GraphPad Prism would, with the same numbers. Every n, test and adjusted P value sits next to the graph; export it as PNG or SVG, or send the table to PlotMate.

Validated on public data it was never built on

Software that counts your cells has to be right. FlowMate's results are checked against independent tools on published, CC-BY data sets — with the protocol and pass criteria written before any result existed, and failures reported as found.
